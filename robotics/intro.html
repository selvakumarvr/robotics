⚡ What is Electricity?

Tiny invisible “somethings” (electrons) flowing through wires — a lot like water flowing through pipes.

Imagine a battery is a water pump, and the wires are pipes. The pump pushes water through the pipes, around a loop, and back. That’s exactly what happens with electricity — except instead of water, super-tiny electrons are moving.

Slide 3 of 8 · Build without solder

🧱 The Breadboard — Your Best Friend

A plastic board with hidden metal strips inside. Poke wires and parts into the holes, and they connect automatically — no soldering needed!

How a breadboard “thinks”

Every breadboard hides two kinds of metal strips inside the plastic. Once you know them, wiring becomes easy.

Power rails (top & bottom): the long red and blue lines along the edges. All holes in one rail are linked, side to side. Red = +, blue/black = −.
Terminal rows (the middle): short columns of 5 holes are linked to each other. Plug your LED, resistor and wire into the same column → they connect!
Center gap: divides the top half from the bottom. Chips (ICs) sit across this gap so each leg gets its own column.
✓ Why we love it: change your wiring 100 times, no damage. Perfect for learning.
Top & bottom: long power rails. Middle: short 5-hole columns separated by a center gap.

🧩 Meet the Components

A friendly tour of the parts you’ll see in nearly every project. Learn these once and you’ll be able to read almost any circuit.

💡
LED
A light that glows when current flows through it.
Long leg = + (anode). Short leg = − (cathode). Polarity matters!
〰️
Resistor
Slows the flow. Protects LEDs from burning out.
Color bands tell you the value in ohms (Ω).
🔘
Pushbutton
Connects two wires only while you hold it down.
Let go → loop opens, circuit OFF.
🎚️
Potentiometer
A turnable knob. A resistor whose value you can change.
Great for volume, brightness, speed.
☀️
Photoresistor (LDR)
Resistance drops in bright light, rises in the dark.
Perfect for night lights & sun sensors.
🔔
Buzzer
Makes a beep or musical tone when powered.
Vary the frequency → make melodies.
⚙️
Servo Motor
Spins to an exact angle, from 0° to 180°.
Powers robot arms, doors, gates, grippers.
🔌
Jumper Wires
Colorful wires that make any connection in seconds.
Red = +, Black/Blue = −, others = signal.

Slide 5 of 8 · The brain

🧠 Meet Arduino: A Tiny Computer Brain

A small board that you program once, then it does its job — over and over — for years.

📡 Inputs → Brain → Outputs

Arduino is the middleman. It listens to the world through input transducers (sensors, switches, sliders, levers), thinks for a moment using your code, then drives output transducers (motors, buzzers, LEDs, screens). That’s the whole secret.

A person steps on a switch (input) → Arduino reads it → Arduino spins a motor or makes a buzzer sound (output).

What’s on the board?

The Arduino UNO is the most popular learner board. It has just a few important parts you need to know:

🔌 USB port
Plugs into your computer. Sends code and power.
🧠 Chip (ATmega328)
The actual brain that runs your program.
⚡ Power pins (5V, 3.3V, GND)
Power for your sensors, LEDs and motors.
🟢 Digital pins (0-13)
ON or OFF only. Great for LEDs & buttons.
🔵 Analog pins (A0-A5)
A whole range of values. Great for sensors.
🔴 Reset button
Press it to restart your program.
Cool fact: Arduino UNO costs about the same as a pizza, runs on a 9V battery, and can drive lights, motors, screens, robots, music makers… the list never ends.

Slide 6 of 8 · Talking to the world

📡 Digital vs Analog Signals

Arduino has two ways of listening and talking: digital (just on or off) and analog (a whole range). Knowing the difference unlocks every project.

🟦 Digital — ON or OFF

Only two values, like a light switch.
  • HIGH = 5V = ON = 1
  • LOW = 0V = OFF = 0
  • Used for: buttons, LEDs, switches
  • Code: digitalRead(2) · digitalWrite(13, HIGH)

🟪 Analog — a Whole Range

Smooth, in-between values like a dimmer knob.
  • Reads 0 to 1023 (1024 steps)
  • Used for: knobs, light sensors, mic, temperature
  • Code: analogRead(A0)
  • Maps to voltage: 0 → 0V · 1023 → 5V
✨ PWM — “fake” analog output
Digital pins are only ON/OFF — but on PWM pins (~3, ~5, ~6, ~9, ~10, ~11) Arduino flickers the pin so fast (≈1000 times a second) that an LED looks dimmer. That trick is called PWM.

analogWrite(9, 128) = pin 9 at half brightness (range 0–255).
Slide 7 of 8 · Tell it what to do

💻 Code = A Recipe for the Brain

You write a tiny program, send it over USB, and Arduino runs it forever. Here’s the classic “Hello World” — Blink.

🔧 Here’s what we’ll wire up

One Arduino UNO. One LED. One 220 Ω resistor. Two jumper wires. That’s the whole hardware — the code on the right makes it blink on and off forever.

Arduino pin 13 → 220 Ω resistor → LED long leg (+). LED short leg (−) → Arduino GND. That’s the entire circuit!
// 🌟 Blink: turn an LED on and off forever

void setup() {
  // runs ONCE when Arduino starts
  pinMode(13, OUTPUT);
}

void loop() {
  // runs FOREVER, top to bottom
  digitalWrite(13, HIGH); // ON
  delay(1000);             // wait 1s
  digitalWrite(13, LOW);  // OFF
  delay(1000);             // wait 1s
}
1
setup() runs once at start-up. Here we tell pin 13 that we’ll be sending power out of it (an OUTPUT).
2
loop() runs over and over forever. That’s how Arduino keeps doing its job.
3
digitalWrite(13, HIGH) = pin 13 ON. HIGH means 5 volts. The LED lights up.
4
delay(1000) = pause for 1000 milliseconds (1 second). Without this, the LED would flash too fast to see!
5
Then digitalWrite(13, LOW) turns it back OFF (LOW = 0 volts) and we wait again. Forever. 💫
