Electronics
Basics

Understanding electricity, circuits, and key components

What is Electronics?

Electronics is the art of controlling the flow of electricity to make things happen — lights blink, music plays, robots move!

Everything around you uses electronics: your phone, your TV, your microwave — they all use tiny circuits to do their jobs.

Think of it like water pipes: Electricity flows through wires like water through pipes. Components control how much flows and where it goes.
📱
Your Phone
Billions of tiny electronic switches
🎮
Game Controller
Buttons, rumble motors, wireless chips
🚗
Smart Car
Sensors, motors, microcontrollers

Key Components You'll Use

What is a
Microcontroller?

The tiny brain that reads sensors and controls the world

A Tiny Computer — On One Chip!

A microcontroller is a small computer on a single chip. It has a processor, memory, and programmable pins — all in one tiny package.

👀

Read Inputs

Sensors: buttons, temperature, light, distance

⚙️

Process Information

Run your code to make decisions

🔊

Control Outputs

LEDs, motors, buzzers, displays, servos

🤖

Found in everyday things!

Traffic Lights Washing Machines Microwaves TV Remotes Smart Watches Cars
Arduino uses the ATmega328P microcontroller chip — an 8-bit processor running at 16 MHz. Slow compared to a phone, but perfect for blinking LEDs!

Meet the Arduino Uno

📌

Read/write HIGH or LOW (on/off). Pins 0-13.

〰️

6 Analog Pins (A0–A5)

Read values between 0 and 1023 from sensors.

⚡

Power Pins

5V, 3.3V, and GND (ground) to power your circuits.

💾

USB Port

Upload your code and power the board from your computer.

🔵

Arduino Uno R3 — the most popular board for beginners

Built-in LED on Pin 13! You can blink it without any extra wires.

Tinkercad

Simulate circuits online — no hardware needed to start!

Get Tinkercad Ready — 5 Steps

1

Go to tinkercad.com

Open any browser — Chrome, Firefox, or Edge. No download needed!

2

Click "Join Now" or "Sign In"

Create a free Autodesk account with your email, or sign in with Google.

3

Click "Circuits" in the sidebar

This is where we'll build our circuits — not 3D design, not Codeblocks.

4

Click "Create new Circuit"

A new blank workspace opens — your digital workbench!

5

Explore the interface

Canvas (middle), Components panel (right), Start Simulation button (top right).

Tinkercad is free forever for students. You can share circuits with your teacher by clicking the "Share" button!

Part 4 — First Project

LED Blink
in Tinkercad!

Wire a circuit, write code, and watch your LED blink — all in your browser

Step-by-Step: Wire Your LED Circuit

1

Add Arduino Uno

Search "Arduino Uno" in the components panel → drag it onto the canvas.

2

Add a Resistor (220Ω)

Drag a resistor onto the canvas. Click it → change value to 220 Ohms.

3

Add an LED

Drag an LED onto the canvas. Anode (+) long leg, Cathode (−) short leg.

4

Connect the wires

Pin 13 → Resistor → LED Anode (+) → LED Cathode (−) → GND on Arduino

Circuit Diagram

Arduino Pin 13
   ↓
Resistor 220Ω
   ↓
LED Anode (+)
   ↓
LED Cathode (−)
   ↓
Arduino GND
Why 220Ω? Arduino gives 5V. LED needs ~2V at 20mA. (5−2) / 0.02 = 150Ω. We use 220Ω for safety!

The Blink Code — Explained!

// setup() runs once when Arduino starts
void setup() {
  pinMode(13, OUTPUT);
  // Pin 13 will control the LED
}

// loop() runs forever, over and over
void loop() {
  digitalWrite(13, HIGH); // LED ON
  delay(1000);            // wait 1 sec
  digitalWrite(13, LOW);  // LED OFF
  delay(1000);            // wait 1 sec
}

setup()

Runs once at the start. Use it to set pin modes and initialize things.

loop()

Runs forever in a loop. Your main logic goes here.

pinMode(13, OUTPUT)

Tells Arduino that pin 13 will send power out.

digitalWrite(13, HIGH/LOW)

Turns the pin ON (5V) or OFF (0V).

delay(1000)

Pause for 1000 milliseconds = 1 second.

In Tinkercad: click "Code" → choose "Text" → paste the code above → click "Start Simulation"!
